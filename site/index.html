Can a model predict my taste?

Partly. I compared 11 models with 5-fold cross-validation repeated 10 times. A baseline that predicts my average for every film is off by 0.91 stars; the best models get to about 0.65. With nested cross-validation, which also accounts for picking the model, the error is 0.66.

After a multiple-comparison correction, none of Random Forest, XGBoost or LightGBM beats a two-variable linear model that only uses the release year and the TMDB rating. A four-variable ordinal logistic regression lands in the same place and tells me one more thing: when the TMDB rating goes up by 0.69 points, the odds of me giving a higher rating multiply by 5.1.

Predict any film

I ran the model on the 5,000 most-voted films on TMDB, the films I have rated and my watchlist. Type a title and it will guess the rating I would give. For a film I have rated, you see the prediction the model made without ever seeing that film next to my actual rating.

The model learned from films I chose to watch, so it is less reliable for the kinds of films I rarely watch. It is off by about half a star on average.

    Should we watch it together?

    I fit the same model on Elif's ratings. Type a title to see both predictions side by side. Elif rates about half a star more generously than me, so I don't compare the two numbers directly: "for both of us" looks at how high the film ranks among each of our predictions and takes the worse of the two.

    Elif's model also leans mostly on the TMDB rating and the film's age; its error (RMSE) is 0.71, mine 0.65. For a film one of us has rated, the prediction comes from a model that never saw that film.
